# ISPFMRA Land Values and Lease Trends: Classes, Regions, and Benchmarks

A guide to the Illinois Society of Professional Farm Managers and Rural Appraisers' annual expert-opinion report — its productivity classes, regions, per-acre value and cash-rent benchmarks, and how it differs from USDA NASS survey data.

**Data current as of:** 2025 ISPFMRA report (released March 27, 2025; covers 2024 land-year sales/leases), the ISPFMRA August 2025 mid-year member survey, and the 2026 ISPFMRA report figures as retrospectively cited in farmdoc daily (July 2026; covers 2025 land-year sales).
**Built/verified:** 2026-07-12

## What the report is

The Illinois Society of Professional Farm Managers and Rural Appraisers (ISPFMRA), working with University of Illinois farmdoc, publishes *Illinois Farmland Values and Lease Trends* each March (unveiled at the Illinois Land Values Conference), plus a smaller mid-year member survey each August focused on cash-rent expectations for the coming year (ISPFMRA, *Land Prices Holding Steady-to-Up — Survey Results*, March 21, 2024, https://ispfmra.org/2024/03/21/land-prices-holding-steady-to-up-survey-results/; AgriNews, *Midyear survey finds softened farmland prices*, September 10, 2025, https://www.agrinews-pubs.com/business/2025/09/10/midyear-survey-finds-softened-farmland-prices/).

**Methodology — expert opinion, not a probability survey.** ISPFMRA members (practicing farm managers and rural appraisers) in each of the state's 10 reporting regions compile actual sales and lease transactions they observed among their own clients and comparable sales during the prior year, aggregate them into four soil-productivity classes per region, and publish the result as a booklet with regional narrative commentary. An academic comparison of farmland price/rent survey instruments characterizes this as an appraiser-panel process feeding "the Society's land value effort that produces a land value booklet each year," structurally distinct from a probability sample (B.J. Deaton, N. Bannon, A. Scholz, and J. Naylor, *Farmland Price-Rent Surveys: Opportunities for Outreach and Teaching*, Applied Economics Teaching Resources, Vol. 7, Iss. 3, June 2025 (published online March 5, 2025), https://www.aetrjournal.org/volumes/volume-7-2025/volume-7-issue-3-june-2025/extension-education/farmland-price-rent-surveys-opportunities-for-outreach-and-teaching).

This contrasts with two other measurement systems that this knowledge base tracks separately (see [SOURCING.md](../SOURCING.md), rule 4, on never mixing these silently):

| System | Who collects it | What it measures | Segmented by soil class? |
|---|---|---|---|
| ISPFMRA *Land Values and Lease Trends* | Appraiser/farm-manager panel, 10 regions | Expert opinion anchored to transactions/leases the panelist personally observed | Yes — Excellent/Good/Average/Fair |
| USDA NASS *Land Values* (June Area Survey, released each August) | Statistically representative probability sample of farm operators | Operator-reported opinion of what land would sell for; covers all "land in farms" (cropland, pasture, other) | No — single statewide/county averages |
| USDA NASS *Cash Rents* (released each August) | Probability sample by county | Reported rents; ~9,289 Illinois recipients in the 2025 survey wave | No — county averages only |
| Auction-result compilations / brokerage transaction data | Selected sample (sellers who expect competition) | Actual closed sale prices, skewed toward stronger tracts | Varies |

NASS's Illinois farm-real-estate and cropland values are covered in [02-land-values-and-price-history.md](02-land-values-and-price-history.md); NASS county cash rents in [03-cash-rents.md](03-cash-rents.md). This document covers only the ISPFMRA series.

## Land productivity classes

ISPFMRA classifies farmland into four classes using the University of Illinois Productivity Index (PI), a soil-productivity score built from USDA-NRCS soil survey data and scaled roughly 0–147, correlating to expected long-run average corn yield under typical management:

| Class | PI range | Typical corn yield |
|---|---|---|
| Excellent | 133–147 | >230 bu/acre |
| Good | 117–132 | 210–230 bu/acre |
| Average | 100–116 | 190–210 bu/acre (non-irrigated) |
| Fair | <100 | <190 bu/acre |

*Source: Illinois Agriculture Authority, summary of farmdoc/ISPFMRA methodology, https://illinoisagricultureauthority.com/illinois-farmland-values (accessed July 2026); the exact bu/ac-to-PI mapping was independently reconfirmed via two separately-worded checks, per this document's underlying verification pass (farmweeknow.com, https://www.farmweeknow.com/profitability/survey-farmland-values-softening/article_4aa96085-57f9-4673-a39b-f728d6e5e205.html). One secondary extraction cited "PI 127–147" for Excellent instead of 133–147; the 133 cutoff recurred independently across two sources and is used here, but neither figure has been checked against the primary ISPFMRA PDF, which was inaccessible in this build environment (see [SOURCING.md](../SOURCING.md), "Build-environment disclosure"). [Not confirmed against a primary source at build time.]*

Worked example: a tract with PI = 138 in Region 7 would be reported in that region's Excellent-class average; one recent report year's Region 7 Excellent-class range was cited at $15,750–$19,800/acre with an average PI of 138.0 (source-derived example; region-level figure not independently re-verified — [not confirmed against a primary source at build time]).

## Regions

ISPFMRA divides Illinois into 10 numbered reporting regions, each compiled by that region's professional members — confirmed across multiple independent sources (AgriNews, *Regional observations in farmland values report* / *Tidbits from farmland values report*, April 8, 2025, https://www.agrinews-pubs.com/business/2025/04/08/regional-observations-in-farmland-values-report/). Partial county assignments recovered in research:

- **Region 6:** Christian, DeWitt, Logan, Macon, Moultrie, Piatt, Shelby counties.
- **Region 7:** Calhoun, Cass, Greene, Jersey, Macoupin, Menard, Montgomery, Morgan, Sangamon, Scott counties.

A complete list of all 10 regions' directional names and county boundaries could not be confirmed against a citable primary source in this build and is omitted rather than guessed. **[Not confirmed against a primary source at build time — treat any "Region 1 = Northeast … Region 10 = Southern" listing found elsewhere as unverified until checked against the primary report's region map at** https://ispfmra.org/land-values-archive/**.]**

In the 2024 report cycle (2023 sales), Regions 3 and 8 posted sharp value increases while Regions 1 and 6 declined — illustrating the wide regional dispersion the statewide averages below can mask (ISPFMRA, March 21, 2024, https://ispfmra.org/2024/03/21/land-prices-holding-steady-to-up-survey-results/).

### Long-run comparison: ISPFMRA Excellent class vs. NASS Illinois farm real estate, 2020–2025

| Year | ISPFMRA Excellent $/acre | NASS Illinois farm real estate $/acre |
|---|---|---|
| 2020 | $10,695 | ~$7,300 |
| 2023 | $16,779 (+57% vs. 2020) | ~$9,300 (+27% vs. 2020) |
| 2024 | ~$15,846–$16,359 (declined; see tables above) | $8,700 |
| 2025 | $15,984 (−5% vs. 2023 peak) | $8,930 (+2.6% vs. 2024) |

*Source: farmdoc daily, "Farmland Prices and Government Programs," July 7, 2026 ((16):118), https://farmdocdaily.illinois.edu/2026/07/farmland-prices-and-government-programs.html, citing the ISPFMRA report series and USDA NASS, *Land Values 2025 Summary*, August 1, 2025, https://www.nass.usda.gov/Publications/Todays_Reports/reports/land0825.pdf. Percentage arithmetic reconfirmed by hand during verification: $10,695→$16,779 = +56.9%; $16,779→$15,984 = −4.7%; $7,300→$9,300 = +27.4%; $9,300→$8,930→$9,850 = +5.9% (2023→2025). Note the farmdoc article's own 2023 Excellent figure ($16,779) differs by ~0.8% from the ISPFMRA 2025 report's stated 2023 figure ($16,906) in the first table — a minor cross-vintage revision, flagged per rule 6 rather than resolved. The much smaller NASS swing (+27% vs. +57%, 2020–2023) reflects that NASS's figure is an all-land average across the whole state, while ISPFMRA's Excellent class is the highest-productivity subset only — see [02-land-values-and-price-history.md](02-land-values-and-price-history.md) for the full NASS series.*

NASS's complementary 2025 statewide figures: Illinois cropland $9,850/acre (+3.1% from 2024) and Illinois pasture $4,200/acre (+5.8%); U.S. cropland $5,830/acre (+4.7%) and U.S. all-land average $4,350/acre (+4.3%) (USDA NASS, *Land Values 2025 Summary*, August 1, 2025, https://www.nass.usda.gov/Publications/Todays_Reports/reports/land0825.pdf).

## Cash rents by class

| Class | 2023 | 2024 range ($/acre) | 2025 range ($/acre) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Excellent | avg $412 | $350–$425 (avg $400) | $315–$404 |
| Good | — | — | $300–$400 (variable cash rent) |
| Average | — | $283–$355 | $260–$342 |
| Fair | — | ~$221 | direction unresolved — see note below |

*Source: ISPFMRA, March 21, 2024 report ("the $400 per acre cash rent for 2024 was $12 lower than the $412 per acre cash rent in 2023"), https://ispfmra.org/2024/03/21/land-prices-holding-steady-to-up-survey-results/; and Farm Progress, "Illinois farmland values and cash rents soften in 2025 ISPFMRA report," https://www.farmprogress.com/farm-business/illinois-farmland-values-soften-cash-rents-follow-suit-in-2025-report. Excellent and Average ranges were independently reconfirmed across three separate checks during verification. Some premium, well-drained Excellent ground reportedly still commanded $450–$500/acre in 2025 (same source).*

**Fair-land rent direction is disputed and not resolved here.** An earlier research draft stated Fair-land cash rent fell from $221 (2024) to ~$198 (2025), a ~10% decline paralleling the other three classes. This document's verification pass found conflicting evidence indicating Fair-land rent may have moved the *opposite* direction — rising while Excellent, Good, and Average fell — but could not confirm exact 2025 Fair-land dollar figures against a primary source. **[Not confirmed against a primary source at build time; treat the $221→$198 figure with caution, and do not assume Fair-land rent tracks the other three classes' direction.]**

## Lease-type mix

Three distinct survey waves reported different lease-type breakdowns; they are not reconciled into a single figure because they may reflect different questions (a manager's whole book of business vs. newly negotiated leases only vs. a separate mid-year wave):

| Source / wave | Fixed cash rent | Variable/flex cash rent | Crop share | Modified share | Custom/other |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 annual report (primary figure) | 41% | 26% | 21% | — | ~12% |
| 2025 annual report, alternate extraction (possibly "newly negotiated" leases only — unreconciled) | 35% | 27% | 27% | — | 6% + 6% |
| Mid-year 2025 member survey (separate wave, reported September 2025) | 25% | 34% | 21% | 14% | 6% |

*Sources: Farm Progress, https://www.farmprogress.com/farm-business/illinois-farmland-values-soften-cash-rents-follow-suit-in-2025-report; the 41/26/21/12 breakdown was independently corroborated by University of Illinois Extension's Farm Focus blog, "2025 Illinois Farmland Values: Statewide and Local Perspectives, Part One," April 11, 2025, https://extension.illinois.edu/blogs/farm-focus/2025-04-11-2025-illinois-farmland-values-statewide-and-local-perspectives-part-one — a second, independent secondary source giving the identical figures, which is why it is presented as the primary reading. The alternate 35/27/27/6/6 breakdown remains unreconciled; AgriNews, "Midyear survey finds softened farmland prices," September 10, 2025, https://www.agrinews-pubs.com/business/2025/09/10/midyear-survey-finds-softened-farmland-prices/, for the mid-year wave.*

| Category | 2023 avg $/acre (2024 report) | % change y/y |
|---|---|---|
| Recreational | $5,250 | +7% |
| Transitional (development-fringe) | $35,000 | +22% |

*Source: Farmweeknow, "Illinois farmland remains a hot commodity," reporting the March 21, 2024 ISPFMRA release, https://www.farmweeknow.com/profitability/illinois-farmland-remains-a-hot-commodity/article_3819a0e2-e7b7-11ee-8520-dfe956dee03b.html. Independently reconfirmed verbatim during verification.*

The 2025 report's regional commentary noted that recreational and "average" quality land showed unexpected strength or stability across regions even as row-crop-grade land softened — quoted by one contributor as "average and recreational land all showed strength or stability across all of the regions. That was an interesting data point that quite honestly a lot of us didn't expect" (ISPFMRA report release coverage, March 27, 2025, https://ispfmra.org/2025/03/27/download-now-our-2025-land-values-lease-trends-report/). A matching 2024-land-year dollar figure for recreational/transitional land (paralleling the $5,250/$35,000 figures above) was not recovered in research and is not reported here. **[Not confirmed against a primary source at build time.]**

## Forward-looking indicators

- **Mid-year 2025 member survey** (recapped by AgriNews, September 10, 2025, https://www.agrinews-pubs.com/business/2025/09/10/midyear-survey-finds-softened-farmland-prices/): estimated Illinois farmland values softened from January 1 to July 1, 2025 by roughly 2.2% (Excellent), 3.5% (Good and Average), and 3% (Fair); for 2026, respondents expected Excellent-class cash rent to be cut by about $17/acre (−4.3%) versus 2025, with half expecting flat rents and half a decrease, and none expecting an increase at that wave. These figures come from a single secondary source and were not independently re-verified in this build's verification pass. **[Not confirmed against a primary source at build time.]**
- **farmdoc daily, April 13, 2026** ("Illinois Cash Rents and Leasing Expectations Through 2027," G. Schnitkey and C. Tsay, https://farmdocdaily.illinois.edu/2026/04/illinois-cash-rents-and-leasing-expectations-through-2027.html): ties the ISPFMRA survey directly into farmdoc's leasing outlook. Among farm managers surveyed, 67% expect 2027 cash rents unchanged from 2026, 9% expect increases, and 24% expect declines; landlord net cash-rent returns compressed slightly from 2024 to 2025, but 2026 rents held steady with marginal increases on the most productive land. Confirmed independently during verification via two separately-worded checks.

## How to obtain the report

The full ISPFMRA *Illinois Farmland Values and Lease Trends* report (current and archive editions back to 2005) is distributed by ISPFMRA itself, not reproduced here in full — it is a member-organization publication and this knowledge base treats it as manifest-only per its sourcing rules (see [../sources/MANIFEST.md](../sources/MANIFEST.md), items 10a–10d).

- Report archive index: https://ispfmra.org/land-values-archive/
- 2025 edition announcement: https://ispfmra.org/2025/03/27/download-now-our-2025-land-values-lease-trends-report/
- Some recent editions have reportedly been offered as free downloads directly from ISPFMRA (e.g., the 2025 edition was noted as "available early and at no cost" per one search snippet); confirm current availability and any cost at the archive index above.

University of Illinois farmdoc and Illinois Extension publish free secondary summaries and analysis shortly after each release, which are a practical substitute for readers without ISPFMRA membership:
