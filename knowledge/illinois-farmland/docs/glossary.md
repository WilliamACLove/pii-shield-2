# Glossary

An alphabetized reference to the terms of art used across this knowledge base on Illinois farmland pricing, sales, and rent — for readers who need working fluency fast.

**Built:** 2026-07-12

---

### 1031 Exchange

A federal tax provision (Internal Revenue Code Section 1031) allowing an owner of investment or business real property, including farmland, to defer capital-gains tax by reinvesting sale proceeds into a "like-kind" replacement property within statutory timelines (45 days to identify, 180 days to close). It is a transaction-structuring tool, not a valuation or pricing concept, but it materially affects who can be a competitive buyer in the Illinois sales market — 1031 sellers under deadline pressure are a recognized source of demand at auctions and in private-treaty deals. See: 09-sales-market.md

### 735 ILCS 5/9-206

The core Illinois statute governing termination of year-to-year farm tenancies (cash rent, crop share, or livestock share alike): the party ending the tenancy must give the other party **written** notice at least four months before the end of the lease year. Because most Illinois farm leases are presumed to run March 1 to the last day of February absent a contrary written agreement, this requirement is commonly satisfied by notice given on or before November 1. The writing requirement cannot be waived, even where the underlying lease itself was oral. See: 08-leases-law.md

### 735 ILCS 5/9-206.1

A companion provision to 735 ILCS 5/9-206 that addresses how farm-tenancy termination notice works when the landlord's interest is held as a life estate — i.e., when the life tenant's death (or other termination of the life estate) intersects with the normal notice timeline. Relevant to estate-transferred farms, which make up the majority of Illinois farmland sales. See: 08-leases-law.md

### Agricultural Economic Value (AEV)

The statutory basis for Illinois farmland property-tax assessment under 35 ILCS 200/10-110 et seq.: for each Bulletin 810 soil Productivity Index class, the Illinois Department of Revenue certifies an AEV equal to a five-year average of (gross income minus production costs) divided by a five-year average Federal Land Bank/Farm Credit mortgage interest rate. The certified Equalized Assessed Value (EAV) is 33.33% of this AEV. AEV is a pure income-capitalization calculation (Value = NOI ÷ rate) but is entirely distinct from, and generally far below, market sale value. See: 10-tax-assessment.md

### AgLetter (Seventh District Agricultural Letter)

A quarterly publication (February, May, August, November) from the Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago reporting farmland value and cash rent changes across its Seventh District, which includes Illinois along with Indiana, Iowa, Michigan, and Wisconsin. AgLetter is based on a survey of agricultural bankers' opinions on recent land-market conditions, making it a third measurement system alongside USDA NASS (operator-reported survey) and ISPFMRA (appraiser/farm-manager expert opinion) — the three should never be treated as interchangeable or averaged together. See: 11-macro-drivers.md

### ARC/PLC (Agriculture Risk Coverage / Price Loss Coverage)

Federal farm-program payment mechanisms under the Farm Bill that provide income support to crop producers when revenue or prices fall below program-set benchmarks. Cited by farmdoc researchers as a key reason Illinois cash rents and farmland values have been "sticky" downward even as farmer returns on cash-rented ground have turned negative — federal support buffers realized farm income and slows the market's adjustment to weaker underlying economics. See: 11-macro-drivers.md

### Auction (Public Auction)

The sale of farmland to the highest bidder at a publicly announced event, either as a single tract or (in a multi-parcel auction) as several tracts offered individually, in combination, or as a whole. Auction has been Illinois's leading single sale channel in recent high-price years, though its share relative to private treaty rises and falls with the market cycle — auctions attract more of the selling activity when sellers expect strong competitive bidding. Fall, after harvest brings yield and price certainty plus liquidity, is traditionally the heaviest auction season. See: 09-sales-market.md

### Bonus Rent (Cash Rent with Bonus)

A flexible cash rent structure (formalized by University of Illinois farmdoc, Schnitkey 2011, periodically refined) that sets a base rent from expected price and trend/APH yield, then compares that base to a formula applied to the actual harvest-time price and yield; if the actual-year calculation exceeds the base rent, the landlord receives the difference as a bonus on top of the base. Distinct in mechanics from the "trigger revenue" flexible-rent model, which the two should never be conflated. See: 08-leases-law.md

### Bulletin 811 / Bulletin 810

The two companion University of Illinois soil-productivity publications that anchor Illinois farmland economics. **Bulletin 811**, "Optimum Crop Productivity Ratings for Illinois Soils" (Olson and Lang, 2000, with its core table revised in 2012), rates soils under *optimum-level* management (top-tier farmer yields) and is the PI basis used by ISPFMRA, farmdoc rent formulas, and the private market. **Bulletin 810** rates the same soils under *average-level* management and is the PI basis Illinois counties have been statutorily required to use for property-tax assessment since assessment year 2006. The two bulletins produce different PI values for the same soil, so a PI number is meaningless without knowing which bulletin generated it. See: 06-soil-productivity-index.md

### Capitalization Rate (Cap Rate)

The rate of return, r, used to convert a stream of net income into a present value under the identity Value = NOI ÷ r; in practice for farmland it is empirically proxied by the observed rent-to-value (current return) ratio. University of Illinois farmdoc research shows Illinois farmland cap rates compressed from roughly 4-5% in the 1990s to the low-to-mid 2% range by the 2020s — a long secular decline that itself pushes land values up for a given level of rent, and whose reversal (via rising interest rates) is a recognized source of downward price pressure. Not to be confused with the discount rate in a full discounted-cash-flow model, though the two converge under a no-growth assumption. See: 07-valuation-math.md

### Cash Rent

A lease arrangement in which the tenant pays the landlord a fixed dollar amount per acre, agreed before the crop year, regardless of the tenant's actual yield or the crop's actual price. It is the dominant lease type in Illinois by share of leases, though the exact share varies by survey and year. Distinguished from crop share (in which the landlord takes a share of the harvested crop) and from flexible/variable cash rent (in which the fixed amount is adjusted by a formula tied to actual price or yield outcomes). See: 08-leases-law.md

### Cash Rents Survey (NASS)

USDA NASS's annual survey program, mandated by the 2008 Farm Bill and providing county-level cash-rent estimates since 2009, published alongside the Land Values Summary each August. It samples a large number of Illinois farm operators and landlords to produce statewide and county-level average cash rents per acre for cropland (and separately for pasture), but it does not break results out by soil-productivity class the way ISPFMRA does, and county estimates in low-response counties carry meaningful sampling noise. See: 03-nass-cash-rents.md

### Certified Value

The per-acre agricultural-use assessed value that the Illinois Department of Revenue certifies annually, by soil Productivity Index class, for property-tax purposes — the product of the statutory Agricultural Economic Value calculation reduced to the 33.33% assessment level, and subject to a year-over-year change cap tied to the "median cropped soil" (PI 111). Certified values are an assessment-law construct, not a market-value estimate, and lag real farm economics by roughly five to seven years because of the underlying five-year income averaging and the statutory cap. See: 10-tax-assessment.md

### Cost Approach

One of the three standard real-estate appraisal methods — estimating value as the cost to reproduce or replace improvements, less depreciation, plus land value — used in Illinois farmland appraisal mainly where buildings, grain storage, or other structural improvements make up a significant share of a property's value. It plays a minor role for typical unimproved row-crop tracts, where the sales comparison and income approaches dominate. See: 07-valuation-math.md

### Crop Reporting District (CRD)

One of the multi-county geographic subdivisions USDA NASS uses to report certain agricultural statistics, and a variable that appears in farmdoc's published regressions converting a farm's Productivity Index into an estimated county cash rent (the regression coefficient on PI is adjusted by a CRD-specific term). Not to be confused with ISPFMRA's own ten-region geography, which uses a different boundary scheme built around its member farm managers' territories. See: 06-soil-productivity-index.md

### Crop Share

A lease arrangement in which the landlord receives a share of the harvested crop (or its sale proceeds) rather than a fixed cash payment, with the landlord typically also contributing a matching share of certain input costs. Crop share (including "modified" crop-share variants) has historically run at roughly a fifth to a quarter of Illinois leases by various ISPFMRA survey counts, trailing fixed and flexible cash rent as the dominant forms, though its role has been shrinking over time as more owners prefer cash-rent's predictability. See: 08-leases-law.md

### Cropland Value

The USDA NASS survey estimate of what an acre of land used for crop production would sell for under current market conditions, reported as part of the annual Land Values Summary alongside pasture value and the broader "farm real estate value" (all land and buildings). Cropland value is a statewide blended average across all quality tiers and is systematically lower than ISPFMRA's Excellent-class average, because ISPFMRA segments explicitly by soil-productivity class and skews toward higher-quality central-Illinois ground. See: 02-nass-land-values.md

### CSR2 (Corn Suitability Rating 2)

**Iowa's** soil-productivity index, not Illinois's. CSR2 runs on a 5-to-100 scale built specifically from historical corn-yield records and soil characteristics for corn suitability, and is entirely distinct from Illinois's Productivity Index (Bulletin 811/810), which runs roughly 47-to-147 and reflects optimum- or average-management yield potential across multiple crops (corn, soybeans, wheat, oats, sorghum, hay). The two scales are not convertible by any simple formula and commercial soil-mapping platforms carry them as separate, state-specific data layers — never quote a CSR2 number as if it describes Illinois land, or vice versa. See: 06-soil-productivity-index.md

### Current Return

The ratio of a farm's net rental income to its market value in a given year — the empirical, observed analogue of the theoretical capitalization rate. farmdoc reports Illinois current return in the low-to-mid 2% range in the mid-2020s, well below longer-run historical averages and below prevailing 10-Year Treasury yields, a gap that analysts treat as a signal of mild downward pressure on farmland prices rather than as a mechanical trigger for an immediate correction. See: 07-valuation-math.md

### Custom Farming

An arrangement in which a landowner hires an operator to perform specific field operations (planting, spraying, harvesting) for a set fee per acre or per pass, while the landowner retains ownership of the crop and bears the input costs and price/yield risk — the reverse of a typical lease, where the operator bears that risk. Custom farming is a small share of Illinois farmland arrangements by acreage but is noted for its comparatively high returns to the landowner-operator in aggregate. See: 08-leases-law.md

### EAV (Equalized Assessed Value)

The value against which Illinois property tax rates are actually applied, generally set at 33.33% of a property's assessed value (or, for farmland, 33.33% of the certified Agricultural Economic Value rather than of market value). Farmland EAV is therefore a wholly different quantity from market value or from a NASS/ISPFMRA appraisal figure and should never be used as a stand-in for either. See: 10-tax-assessment.md

### Farm Crisis (1980s)

The severe farmland value collapse that followed the 1970s boom, driven by Volcker-era Federal Reserve interest-rate tightening (short rates approaching 20%) after years of inflation-fueled, debt-financed land buying. Illinois farmland fell roughly 40% or more from its 1981 peak to its mid-1980s trough — a somewhat milder decline than some neighboring Corn Belt states — and the episode remains the central cautionary reference point in Illinois farmland commentary whenever interest rates rise and land values plateau, as in the 2023-2026 period. See: 11-macro-drivers.md

### Farm Real Estate Value

USDA NASS's broadest annual land-value measure: the average per-acre value of all land and buildings in farms, combining cropland, pasture, and other farm real estate into a single statewide figure. It is the headline number in NASS's August Land Values Summary and, being a blend across all land uses and quality tiers, sits below both the NASS cropland-only figure and any ISPFMRA class-specific average. See: 02-nass-land-values.md

### farmdoc

The University of Illinois Department of Agricultural and Consumer Economics' public-facing research, extension, and decision-tool platform (farmdoc.illinois.edu and its farmdoc daily blog). farmdoc publishes crop budgets, operator and land return estimates, the flexible cash rent and PI-to-cash-rent formulas used throughout Illinois lease practice, and co-produces the annual ISPFMRA Illinois Farmland Values and Lease Trends report. It ranks just below official statistical series and survey-of-experts reports in this knowledge base's source hierarchy, above general industry and press coverage. See: 05-farmdoc-returns-tools.md

### Farmland Assessment Technical Advisory Board (FATAB)

The Illinois advisory board that works with the Department of Revenue to certify each year's per-Productivity-Index-class Agricultural Economic Value used in farmland property-tax assessment. FATAB's role is technical certification within the statutory formula (35 ILCS 200/10-115 and related sections), not independent rate-setting. See: 10-tax-assessment.md

### Flexible (Variable) Cash Rent

A cash rent arrangement in which the rent paid is not fixed in advance but adjusted, by a pre-agreed formula, based on actual outcomes for crop price and/or yield during the lease year — sitting between fixed cash rent (all risk on the tenant) and crop share (proportional risk-sharing) in how it allocates price/yield risk between landlord and tenant. Illinois practice documents at least two distinct formula families: the farmdoc "cash rent with bonus" model and a separately circulated "trigger revenue" model; the two are not interchangeable and should always be identified by name when discussed. Roughly a quarter to a third of Illinois leases used a flexible/variable structure across recent ISPFMRA survey waves. See: 08-leases-law.md

### Gordon Growth Model

The general form of the income-capitalization identity when expected rental income is assumed to grow at a constant rate g: Value = CF₁ ÷ (r − g), where CF₁ is next period's expected cash flow and r is the capitalization/discount rate. It generalizes the simpler no-growth Value = NOI ÷ r formula and is the theoretical bridge academics (Burt 1986; Featherstone and Baker 1987) use to connect farmland value to expected future rent growth, not just current rent — relevant whenever a valuation needs to account for anticipated cash-rent appreciation rather than treating current rent as a permanent flat stream. See: 07-valuation-math.md

### Gross Acre

The total acreage within a parcel's legal boundary, including tillable cropland as well as non-tillable areas such as waterways, farmstead, timber, ponds, roads, and drainage features. Gross acre figures overstate a tract's productive capacity relative to its tillable acreage, and per-acre prices or rents should always be checked against which acreage base (gross or tillable) they are quoted on before being compared across properties. See: 07-valuation-math.md

### Income Capitalization (Income Approach)

One of the three standard real-estate appraisal methods, and the dominant approach for valuing typical Illinois row-crop farmland: it converts an expected net income stream (NOI) into a present value using a capitalization rate, per the identity Value = NOI ÷ r. In Illinois it is not merely an appraisal convention — the state's statutory farmland property-tax assessment formula (Agricultural Economic Value) is itself a codified income-capitalization calculation, distinct from the appraisal-industry usage of the same method for market-value opinions. See: 07-valuation-math.md

### ISPFMRA

The Illinois Society of Professional Farm Managers and Rural Appraisers. In cooperation with University of Illinois farmdoc, ISPFMRA publishes the annual *Illinois Farmland Values and Lease Trends* report each March (covering the prior year's transactions), plus a mid-year member survey update each August. Unlike USDA NASS's probability-sample surveys, ISPFMRA's report is an expert-opinion/appraiser-panel compilation: professional farm managers and rural appraisers across Illinois's ten regions summarize actual sales and rent transactions they personally observed, categorized into four Productivity-Index-based land classes. This makes ISPFMRA figures closer to the professional market's view of quality-segmented land, but not a census of all sales. See: 04-ispfmra-report.md

### June Area Survey

The USDA NASS data-collection instrument underlying the annual Land Values Summary: an area-sampling frame of roughly 9,000 one-square-mile land segments across the lower 48 states, in which enumerators collect operator-reported (self-reported, opinion-based) estimates of cropland, pasture, and total farm real estate value — not actual transaction or closing prices. This design is the reason NASS land values are described as "what land could be sold for" rather than "what land did sell for," and why they diverge from ISPFMRA and from actual sale prices. See: 02-nass-land-values.md

### Land Classes (Excellent / Good / Average / Fair)

The four soil-productivity tiers ISPFMRA uses to segment Illinois farmland sale prices and cash rents by region, defined by Productivity Index (Bulletin 811) cutoffs: **Excellent** (PI roughly 133-147, corresponding to the highest corn-yield potential), **Good** (roughly 117-132), **Average** (roughly 100-116), and **Fair** (below 100). These classes are the organizing structure of ISPFMRA's report and of farmdoc's PI-based cash-rent formulas, and should not be confused with the Illinois property-tax system's own certified-value schedule, which is indexed to Productivity Index points directly rather than to these four named tiers. See: 04-ispfmra-report.md

### Land Values Summary (NASS)

USDA NASS's annual publication (released each August) reporting state-level average farm real estate value, cropland value, and pasture value, built from the June Area Survey. It is the most widely cited single number for "what is Illinois farmland worth," but is subject to a periodic "final historic revision" after each five-year Census of Agriculture that re-benchmarks prior years' figures — meaning year-over-year percent changes calculated across different report vintages are not always on a consistent base, and apparent declines can sometimes be revision artifacts rather than market moves. See: 02-nass-land-values.md

### Landlord's Lien

A statutory security interest (735 ILCS 5/9-316) granted to Illinois farm landlords over a tenant's crop, lasting six months past the lease's expiration, enforceable by distraint, and given priority over competing UCC Article 9 security interests (e.g., an operating lender's lien on the same crop). It protects the landlord's claim to rent (cash or share) directly out of the crop itself, independent of the tenant's other creditors. See: 08-leases-law.md

### March 1 Lease Year

The default annual period for Illinois farm leases — absent a written agreement stating otherwise — running from March 1 through the last day of February. This convention underlies the timing of the 735 ILCS 5/9-206 four-month termination-notice requirement (making on-or-before-November-1 the typical notice deadline) and the common split of cash rent payments around spring (March) and fall (November) dates, though a shift toward calendar-year leases and full up-front spring payment has become more common on professionally managed farms. See: 08-leases-law.md

### Median Cropped Soil

The reference soil-productivity point, defined statutorily as Productivity Index 111, against which Illinois caps the year-over-year change in certified farmland assessed values. Since Public Act 98-0109 (effective assessment year 2015), no PI class's certified value may rise (or presumably fall) by more than 10% of the prior year's certified value at this PI-111 reference point — a stabilizing mechanism that slows how quickly farmland property-tax assessments respond to swings in farm income. See: 10-tax-assessment.md

### NCREIF Farmland Index

An index of investment returns on institutionally owned (pension-fund and similar) farmland properties across the U.S., maintained by the National Council of Real Estate Investment Fiduciaries. It measures a distinct universe — large, professionally managed, institutionally held farms — from NASS survey values, ISPFMRA appraiser opinions, or county-level transaction prices, and historically shows farmland delivering equity-like long-run returns with markedly lower volatility, though it posted its first-ever annual negative return in 2024 before a marginally positive 2025. Illinois acreage sits inside diversified national farmland REIT and institutional portfolios rather than being tracked as an Illinois-specific NCREIF sub-index. See: 09-sales-market.md

### Net Farm Income

A U.S. Department of Agriculture measure of aggregate farm profitability (revenue less expenses across the whole farm sector or a state), distinct from land-specific measures like NOI or operator/land returns on a single tract. Illinois commentary uses swings in net farm income — a sharp rise around 2021-2022 followed by a steep decline into 2024 — as a key explanatory driver behind the "sticky" gap between falling farmer profitability and farmland prices/rents that have not fallen proportionally. See: 11-macro-drivers.md

### NOI (Net Operating Income)

The net income figure used as the numerator in farmland income-capitalization valuation: cash rent (or crop-share-equivalent income) less property tax, insurance, and management costs attributable to the land. NOI, not gross rent, is the correct input to Value = NOI ÷ r; using gross cash rent in place of NOI overstates implied value or understates the true capitalization rate. See: 07-valuation-math.md

### Operator/Land Returns

farmdoc's annual estimates of the income remaining to, respectively, the farm operator and the landowner after production costs on a representative central-Illinois grain farm — a management-accounting measure distinct from either NOI (used in formal valuation) or net farm income (a sector-wide aggregate). Operator and land returns spiked in 2021-2022 on high grain prices, then swung negative for corn-soybean rotations on cash-rented ground for several consecutive years, a divergence from still-rising or flat land values and cash rents that farmdoc treats as evidence of market "stickiness." See: 05-farmdoc-returns-tools.md

### Price-to-Rent Multiple

The ratio of a farm's price per acre to its annual cash rent per acre — the reciprocal-scaled companion to the rent-to-value (current return) ratio, expressed as a multiple (years of rent) rather than a percentage. A low current return corresponds to a high price-to-rent multiple, and Illinois's multiple has been in the high-30s in the mid-2020s given current-return levels in the low-to-mid 2% range. See: 07-valuation-math.md

### Private Treaty

A negotiated, non-auction sale of farmland directly between a seller (or their broker) and a single buyer, at a price arrived at through private negotiation rather than competitive public bidding. Private treaty and public auction are the two dominant Illinois sale channels, and the mix between them shifts with the cycle — private treaty tends to regain share as auction-market enthusiasm cools. See: 09-sales-market.md

### Productivity Index (PI)

The University of Illinois soil-rating scale (established by Bulletin 811 for optimum management, or Bulletin 810 for average management) that scores Illinois soil map units on their crop-yield potential, roughly 47 to 147 on the Bulletin 811 scale. PI is the backbone of both ISPFMRA's four land classes (Excellent/Good/Average/Fair) and farmdoc's formulas converting a farm's PI directly into an estimated cash rent, and — via the separate Bulletin 810 scale — of Illinois's statutory farmland property-tax assessment. A tract's quoted PI is normally an acreage-weighted average across all soil map units within its boundary, not a single soil series' rating. See: 06-soil-productivity-index.md

### PTAX-203

The Illinois Real Estate Transfer Declaration form (required under 35 ILCS 200/31-1 et seq.) that must be filed with every recorded deed at the county recorder's office, capturing the sale price, parties, and property description for every Illinois real estate transaction, including farmland. PTAX-203 filings are the raw administrative data underlying most farmland transaction-price research and turnover-rate studies (farmdoc, county assessors, and private aggregators all draw on this filing), distinguishing actual closed-sale-price data from NASS's or ISPFMRA's survey/opinion-based figures. See: 09-sales-market.md

### Rent-to-Value Ratio

The ratio of a farm's annual cash rent per acre to its market value per acre, expressed as a percentage — the observed, empirical analogue of the capitalization rate, also called the "current return." Illinois's rent-to-value ratio compressed over decades from roughly 4-5% to the low-to-mid 2% range, a decline that, taken alone (holding rent constant), mechanically implies rising land values, and whose recent widening gap against benchmark interest rates is read as a signal of downward price pressure. See: 07-valuation-math.md

### Sales Comparison Approach

One of the three standard real-estate appraisal methods, and the primary method used for valuing typical unimproved Illinois row-crop tracts: value is estimated from the observed sale prices of comparable nearby properties, adjusted for differences in soil productivity (via Productivity Index), acreage mix (gross vs. tillable), drainage, access, and other characteristics. It is distinct from the income approach, which derives value from capitalized rental income rather than from comparable closed sales. See: 07-valuation-math.md

### Sealed Bid

A sale method in which prospective buyers submit confidential written offers by a stated deadline, with the property awarded to the highest (or otherwise most favorable) bid without the bidders seeing each other's offers — in contrast to a live/public auction, where bids are visible and bidders react to one another in real time. Used less frequently than public auction or private treaty for Illinois farmland but appears periodically, particularly for estate or institutional dispositions. See: 09-sales-market.md

### Soil Map Unit / Soil Series

The basic mapping units of the USDA/NRCS county soil survey system (viewable via Web Soil Survey/SoilWeb), each corresponding to a distinct combination of soil series, slope, and drainage characteristics. Illinois Productivity Index values are assigned at the soil-map-unit level, and a parcel's overall PI (as reported by tools like Surety/AgriData or quoted in a sale listing) is an acreage-weighted average across the map units within the parcel — never a single number describing the whole tract's soil uniformly. High-value Illinois "cash grain belt" ground is dominated by dark prairie soil series such as Drummer (Illinois's official State Soil), Flanagan, Ipava, Sable, and Muscatune. See: 06-soil-productivity-index.md

### Statute of Frauds (740 ILCS 80/2)

The Illinois law under which a lease of farmland for a term of more than one year must be in writing to be enforceable; oral farm leases of one year or less remain enforceable, and Illinois courts treat a longer-running oral tenancy as a series of successive one-year lettings rather than voiding it outright. Distinguished from 735 ILCS 5/9-206's separate requirement that any *termination notice*, whether the underlying lease was oral or written, must itself be in writing. See: 08-leases-law.md

### Tillable Acre

The portion of a farm's total (gross) acreage that is actually suitable for row-crop production, excluding waterways, farmsteads, timber, ponds, roads, and other non-cropped areas. Per-acre sale prices, rents, and productivity figures are frequently — and should always be clearly — quoted on a tillable-acre basis rather than a gross-acre basis, since the two bases can differ substantially and produce misleadingly different implied per-acre values for the same tract. See: 07-valuation-math.md

### Transitional Land

Farmland located near expanding development fringes (urban, suburban, or infrastructure growth areas) whose value reflects anticipated non-agricultural (residential, commercial, industrial) use potential rather than, or in addition to, its row-crop productive capacity. ISPFMRA reports transitional land values separately from its four productivity-based classes precisely because its pricing is driven by development expectations rather than by soil PI or crop income. See: 04-ispfmra-report.md

### Trigger Revenue Lease

A flexible cash rent model, circulated through Extension and industry channels drawing on farmdoc cost budgets, that sets a base rent (commonly around 90% of the prior fixed rent), computes a per-crop "trigger" revenue level (base rent plus non-land costs), and splits any actual revenue above that trigger between landlord and tenant (commonly 50/50). Mechanically distinct from farmdoc's "cash rent with bonus" model — the two flexible-rent families should be named explicitly rather than treated as synonyms. See: 08-leases-law.md

### Turnover Rate (Land Turnover)

The share of total farmland acreage that changes ownership through recorded sale in a given year, derived from county-level PTAX-203 transfer-declaration data. Illinois's statewide average annual turnover has run around 1.5% (implying an average holding period on the order of several decades), varying widely by county with rural, lower-value counties generally turning over faster than land near metropolitan areas. Distinct from — and much lower than — the frequency of lease turnover or renegotiation, which happens annually. See: 09-sales-market.md

### Use-Value Assessment

The property-tax principle, in force in Illinois since the Farmland Assessment Law took effect in 1977 (35 ILCS 200/10-110 et seq.), that qualifying farmland (land farmed for the two preceding years) is assessed based on its capitalized agricultural income-producing capacity rather than on its fair market (highest-and-best-use) value. This is why certified farmland assessed values sit far below, and move on a different schedule from, NASS, ISPFMRA, or actual transaction-price measures of market value — the two systems are answering different questions by statutory design. See: 10-tax-assessment.md
